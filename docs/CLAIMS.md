## Claims that must NOT be made

- Real money movement, production use, "payments platform", "banking-grade". Settlement is **simulated**.
- Any load figure not produced by a recorded run. Benchmarks against the mock LLM must say "mock LLM with simulated latency of X ms".
- "Minimizes" transfers. Debt simplification is a greedy heuristic and **reduces** them (a counterexample where it uses 4 transfers and 3 suffice is pinned in `internal/ledger/transfers_test.go`; on random small inputs it exceeded the true minimum in 4 of 3000 cases).
- PII "removal". Only regex redaction of specific patterns (card numbers, phones, emails) is implemented; names are not redacted.
- Encryption at rest of receipts (envelope encryption was cut from scope).
- Kubernetes, Terraform, autoscaling (README extension path only, not built).
- Vision-model extraction, Flutter, Swift/iOS-native, multi-currency, push notifications.
- "Tested on iOS/Android" for any platform not actually run. Real-camera capture is untested (simulators only).
- Mutation-testing counts or "N mutations caught" figures. During M2 each lock, check and authorization condition was broken on purpose and a test confirmed failing, but with throwaway scripts that are not in the repo, so the counts cannot be reproduced. The reproducible evidence is the tests themselves (rows 7, 13-16).
- Accuracy numbers from synthetic receipts presented as real-world accuracy.
- The `throughput-queue` figure as "what Postgres can do" or as the queue alone: it includes a local HTTP call and an insert per job, and the workers, mock, harness and Postgres share one laptop. Any queue figure for a real LLM, another machine, or more than 4 jobs in flight per worker.
- "Time to resolve an unknown settlement" as a property of a payment provider or of the system in general: it is set by the reconcile schedule constants (placeholders) and the mock provider's delays.
- Real payment processing, charges, or any settlement figure not from a recorded run against the mock provider.
- A throughput, latency or capacity figure from the stress runs (the api, the workers, the mock provider, the harness and Postgres share one machine, and the run is sized for contention, not load). "The protection is necessary" or "unnecessary" from a control that showed no violation: it shows only that this workload did not get past it. That the production build is "safe" or "correct": it kept the listed invariants in 3 runs of this workload.
- Item edits under load, or a negative-control result for the edit lock or the version check, before they are recorded (row 22 covers tests only; the code's stress workload includes edits since M6.2 but the recorded runs, rows 1, 2 and 21, are from before that until 6.3). Negative-control results for any protection that was not actually disabled in the recorded run.
